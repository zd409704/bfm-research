## 2. Critique of FRAMEWORK_REVIEW (theory / implementation / empirical separation; value judgment)

**The strongest contribution is the separation of the causal chain into theory, implementation, and empirical benefit.** The draft identifies conditional controllability, planner access to useful behaviors, replay drift, and retention as distinct uncertainties. It correctly rejects a dimensionality argument for z-space exploration, distinguishes task value from a linear reward readout, and explains why a long-horizon conditional policy may have an unhelpful short prefix. Its most promising benefit—reusing real experience across behavioral conditions—is a reasonable investment hypothesis. Its concern about controllability and task-critic coverage is also well chosen.

Several statements nevertheless need tighter scope:

1. **Sampled relabeling does not train the entire policy family from every transition.** The verdict, §2, and §5 repeatedly describe one transition as training every z or all z. The implemented mixture samples a finite number of conditions. Shared parameters may generalize beyond them, but neither complete coverage nor beneficial transfer follows. L3's theoretical “Yes” also confuses eligibility for an update with expansion of executable capability. A new next-motion frame entering the relabel distribution creates a training opportunity; it does not establish that the frame is controllably reachable from useful starts.

2. **The ideal FB argument is sometimes presented as a property of the learned controller.** L2 says that holding z runs a policy optimal for some reward and consequently produces coherent, stabilized exploration. Reward indexing, learned optimality, and physical stabilization are three separate claims. Finite coverage, approximation error, actor optimization, and the replay distribution intervene. L1's dual-reward interpretation needs the fixed-representation/full-rank or identified-subspace assumptions and residual qualification retained by sealed B01. These qualifications matter precisely during the proposed cold start. This is not a reason to add whitening.

3. **The contact-impossibility claim drops necessary premises.** Section 3(a) says that equal kinematics with different contact forces means z cannot select between placements. Sealed B03 requires equal future feature occupancy and indistinguishable full actor input, with the relevant feasible torque ambiguity. Equal current motion descriptors alone are insufficient. Perceptive actor inputs, different z values, and later occupancy can distinguish behaviors. Likewise, §2's description of contact aliasing as a “known violation” must distinguish a conditional theoretical counterexample from a demonstrated violation on the production plant. `CORRECTED_CONCLUSIONS` preserves these premises better than `FRAMEWORK_REVIEW` does.

4. **Missing independent planner noise is a coverage risk, not a proof that executed exploration is only demo-anchored.** A deterministic planner can produce different z values across states, histories, and checkpoints; expert/near rollouts also produce task-value data. This does not guarantee adequate coverage, but neither does it prove confinement to a thin tube. Statements that randomly relabeled behaviors are “never executed” should say that random-sphere update sampling is not itself an executed exploration source. Actual overlap must be measured. The needed mechanism is sufficient executed coverage, for which planner noise is one candidate intervention.

5. **The value judgment conflates a deployment hurdle with a scientific necessity.** Requiring a 0.67 step ratio can be a legitimate investment criterion, but no argument makes a 33% reduction the boundary between a useful and useless framework. Failure to achieve it on one traversal task does not refute later-task reuse, and a ratio between 0.67 and 1 can still indicate useful improvement. The draft should distinguish “does not clear this investment hurdle,” “evidence of no benefit on this task,” and “mechanism falsified.”

The motion-only reward limitation, partial-observation concern, macro-switching discussion, and actor-drift analysis support cautious continuation. They do not establish that a conditional behavioral library must look like a short-horizon pose-reaching library. That distinction is central to assessing E0.

## 3. Critique of GAP_TABLE and CORRECTED_CONCLUSIONS (scope errors, overclaims, missed corrections)

**The corrections substantially improve the evidence record.** H1–H4 remain open; B01 conditioning results are scoped to artificial distributions; B02 contracts do not establish real macro-Q stability; B03 predictor error is separated from a physics lower bound; B04 cannot establish an optimal horizon or acceleration; and B05 arithmetic/FSM checks do not certify production recovery. The withdrawal of the cross-height waste prescription is particularly important. These are reasons to retain the package and revise it rather than start over.

The following gaps remain:

- **Evidence provenance is too coarse.** `GAP_TABLE` uses “Exists” for owner-confirmed behavior and for baseline specifications in the same column. A baseline is a contract, not a production observation. Each row needs a source pointer and a status such as owner-reported, specified, locally exercised, or externally unverified. The absence of named production classes in a search is narrower evidence than complete absence of equivalent functionality; describe the search result accordingly.

- **The three data paths are not audited explicitly.** The table should distinguish offline pose/velocity material used as current-B cues, real transitions used for FB/actor updates, and real macro history/returns/executed z used for task learning. It should explicitly exclude constructing fictitious off-policy transitions from unindexed frames. These boundaries are more important than adding another exploration knob.

- **P0 prerequisites are inconsistent.** GAP-06 assigns a production BN-order test P1, although §5 of `MINIMAL_EXPERIMENTS` lists it in the production verification plan. Executed-label integrity, update-condition isolation, recurrent recomputation, variable-duration macro targets, numerical-fault rollback, and resume integrity belong before interpretable training comparisons. Passing E0 cannot compensate for a broken collector or target contract.

- **The frozen implementation and proposed diagnostics are not always distinguished.** GAP-03 mixes planner output with the low-level actor's mean-action execution; these are separate stages that should be logged separately. GAP-07 overstates the nonexecution of randomly trained conditions. GAP-17 defers a contact-skill experiment while E0 is described as a real-physics branching gate; clarify what E0 measures and what remains untested.

- **New numerical corrections need exact supporting locations.** The proposed 0.5625 bound is appropriate for equally likely scalar targets of ±0.75 under the stated uninformative-input and per-coordinate mean-square convention. It is not automatically the bound for a summed vector loss, a different target distribution, or production contact control. The correction should identify the source construction and reduction. Likewise, the B04 dimensions, horizons, and reference objective deserve exact source/result pointers. This review accepts the owner's requested demotion of those results; it does not independently certify Draft A's additional script-level details from a synthesis skim.

- **Retained limitations need explicit linkage.** Saying that original review limitations are “kept” is weaker than identifying the relevant surviving premises and integration checks. The contact claim discussed above illustrates the consequence: the corrections table retains strong premises that the framework prose then omits. Add a compact mapping to applicable sealed limitations, without reopening the packets or editing their historical results.

The corrected conclusions should remain an overlay on the sealed record. Neither their existence nor a repaired experiment plan changes an empirical cell from pending to demonstrated.

## 4. Critique of MINIMAL_EXPERIMENTS (thresholds, sequence, missing controls, budget realism)

**The proposed questions are useful, but the thresholds and execution logic do not yet yield reliable verdicts.** Staging A before B/C is defensible as a pragmatic priority. The current plan needs the following repairs before its rules can govern spending or termination.

**E0 measures cue-specific pose proximity, not sufficient behavioral controllability.** Its gate uses demo goals and k=10 despite D1 naming demo or replay goals and the procedure also measuring k=50. Unreachable start/goal pairs, velocity-bearing transit frames, and useful wind-up behavior can fail the metric without invalidating the library. Conversely, modest joint-pose changes could pass while balance, contact control, and task usefulness remain absent. The chosen distance omits velocity despite velocity being part of the motion cues. The “root height proxy,” feature scaling, and mismatched-goal construction are unspecified.

Before giving E0 stop authority, freeze the feature metric and distinguish reachable cue response, repeatable valid branching, and task-useful reachability. Report initial-distance-adjusted progress, both horizons and goal pools, fall/termination rates, and a same-z repeatability or rollout-randomness control. Upright replay starts are reasonable for one stratum, but cannot stand for recovery capability from failed states. Preserve terminated branches in denominators. Specify simulator state restoration, shared randomness, history handling, and whether complete restorable states exist in replay. Snapshotting contact-rich state is an engineering prerequisite, not automatically available from a motion record.

The CR bootstrap must identify whether it resamples seeds, starts, or goals; thousands of correlated branches do not replace independent training seeds. Its pass/fail gap also needs an explicit inconclusive outcome. The deadlock definition conflicts across files: `FRAMEWORK_REVIEW` §4 uses an OR among three indicators, while E0 requires poor CR AND another indicator. Branching diversity can be inflated by uncontrolled falls, and matched action variance alone does not make outcome spread useful exploration.

**E1's censoring rule is a blocking statistical defect.** The common protocol correctly calls nonattainment censored, but the median-ratio decision does not define a censor-aware estimator. If all FW and direct-action seeds fail, substituting S_max produces ratio 1 and triggers REFUTE despite providing no comparison of threshold times. A median may be unidentified when most seeds are censored; the bootstrap log-ratio may then be undefined. Predeclare a bounded estimand that handles nonattainment, or explicitly make such cases inconclusive and report attainment probabilities alongside time-to-threshold evidence. A reliably successful baseline against a failing framework is a different outcome from both failing.

Selecting the “best” direct-action arm using the final data and adding seeds after an interim result both affect uncertainty. Freeze the comparator from pilots or account for selection; specify the sequential decision rule and seed-level resampling. Five seeds and four paired wins are screening evidence, not automatic precision. Clarify whether the claimed effect is merely faster than the comparator or at least 33% faster: the current interval checks the former, while the point threshold suggests the latter. Define the 95% bound convention and acknowledge that repeated Wilson panels are an operational success criterion, not a global confidence guarantee across every training checkpoint.

**Invalid-run handling can bias the comparison.** Automatically restarting and excluding an arm after more than two halts conditions evidence on reliability. An arm-specific failure can disappear from the efficiency comparison, while rollback can hide spent interactions. Require fault inspection and a documented recovery disposition; preserve every scheduled seed and failure in the accounting. Separate scientific nonattainment, implementation failure, and unresolved invalidity. Log attempted, committed, repeated-after-rollback, evaluation, and probe transitions, plus their compute costs. Valid physical failures must remain committed experience. “Committed steps” alone is not total interaction cost.

**The controls do not fully match the stated questions.** DA-C adds correlated noise but does not implement the action-repeat/macro-decision null promised in `FRAMEWORK_REVIEW` §10. Thus E1 cannot claim to isolate both temporal coherence and shorter task credit assignment. Conversely, holding direct actions for K steps reduces feedback frequency, so even an added repeat arm needs careful interpretation against a closed-loop conditional actor. Freeze the exact control family, observation/history access, reward access, capacity, update-to-data ratio, and tuning selection endpoint. Equal numbers of configurations do not by themselves equalize compute or engineering effort.

FW receives demo cues and DA does not. That can be a valid comparison of complete methods with different information use, but it is not a clean attribution of gains to hierarchy or relabeling alone. TR helps answer the requested imitation comparison, yet its reference phase, recovery behavior, and adaptation across heights are undefined. It is neither optional nor deferred in E1's table, whereas `FRAMEWORK_REVIEW` §10 says alternatives after its first three rows are deferred. Resolve that conflict and label the information budget explicitly.

**The sequence can prematurely close the identified repair path.** Draft A identifies lack of planner exploration as a major objection, then tests FW with that knob off and forbids E2/C after E1 refutation at production scale. A failure of the existing schedule cannot settle whether the proposed exploration repair works. Keep any conclusion scoped to the tested configuration and permit a separately capped diagnostic of the suspected failure, without committing to the full E2 sweep. Deferring C is acceptable, but later-task reuse must then remain untested rather than be rejected by E1.

**E2 and C are outlines rather than complete preregistrations.** E2 must choose held noise or a specified correlated process, match source timing and applied-action perturbations after clipping, and address changing variance as the actor learns. No detected near effect should be called “unresolved,” unless an equivalence rule supports “neutral.” Z0 reuse is valid only with identical protocol and seed accounting; the E1 extra-seed branch currently complicates that reuse.

C's return filter changes buffer turnover, transition age, update reuse, and the behavior policy's later data collection. It can assess the total effect of filtering, but cannot by itself attribute gains to previously irrelevant experience. A bounded causal diagnostic could share a tagged collection stream and match eligible update budgets before the later-task comparison. Define the running-median window and filtering unit, track which low-return transitions actually enter updates, and freeze the regression reference. State phase caps, seeds, costs, and nonattainment rules. The readable/executable/findable distinction is valuable; the current executable measure inherits E0's limitations.

**The proposed budget is not demonstrably small.** At the stated caps, E1 is 20 × 150M = **3.0 billion committed control steps**. Its pilots add up to **720 million**. The one permitted seed expansion adds **1.5 billion**, and E2 adds **3.0 billion**. That is up to **8.22 billion** before C, evaluation, probing, invalid recovery, and any optional reference arm. E0's binding check occurs at 75M steps per FW seed—375M across five seeds—rather than before substantial training spend. The baseline runs may already be underway by then.

The draft honestly labels relative costs as estimates, but calling E0 nearly free and the program bounded small scale still requires measured throughput, a wall-clock/compute cap, and an actual staged launch order. Define what “control step” counts across vector environments; otherwise the 100k source ramp, 2M evaluation interval, and 150M budget may be incomparable. Account for evaluation episodes and the 128-way branch probes explicitly. Choose a smaller first decision if the measured budget cannot fit the intended bounded investment.

Finally, the secondary Q-calibration metric needs a consistent target. A bootstrapped task Q is not generally calibrated against one macro's immediate return alone. Specify a full-return estimate or compatible bootstrap, continuation policy, terminal handling, and replay-age assignment. A slope in [0.7, 1.3] without intercept/error diagnostics is insufficient. The framework calls this a gate while E1 calls it non-gating; choose one role. Actor KL also needs an explicit stochastic policy distribution, or a different drift measure for deterministic outputs.

## 5. Agreement and disagreement with Draft A headline judgment

**I agree with “revise-then-continue, at bounded small scale” as the current investment posture.** The architecture has a coherent conditional case for improving experience utilization, and the absence of a full neural convergence proof is not grounds for rejection. No provided evidence establishes practical sample efficiency, stable joint learning, or reusable contact behaviors on the home-humanoid plant. The package correctly refuses full-scale endorsement.

I disagree with treating short-horizon demo-pose CR as a necessary test of the entire behavioral library, with treating missing planner noise as proof of a demo-tube restriction, and with making a 0.67 time ratio on one task the scientific boundary of framework value. These are proposed operational choices that require narrower claims and better controls.

The strongest reason to continue is that reward-independent reuse may make expensive real transitions useful across later goals. The strongest objection is that update eligibility may never translate into stable, discoverable, task-useful behaviors under joint drift. A valid bounded test should distinguish those possibilities. The present protocol could instead mistake metric mismatch, censoring, or implementation faults for a decisive answer.

## 6. Mandatory fixes before adjudication (labeled [CRITICAL], [MAJOR], [MINOR])

- **[CRITICAL] F1 — Repair nonattainment and uncertainty rules.** Define censor-aware outcomes, both-fail cases, seed resampling, comparator selection, and the single seed-extension procedure. No fabricated threshold time may trigger refutation. Distinguish the investment effect-size hurdle from the uncertainty claim.
- **[CRITICAL] F2 — Replace invalid-run exclusion with an auditable fault protocol.** Require inspection before recovery, retain all scheduled seeds and reliability outcomes, preserve valid physical failures, and include rollback/restart work in costs. Make production collector/target/recovery contract verification a prerequisite.
- **[CRITICAL] F3 — Limit E0's stop authority to a validated construct.** Freeze metric/reachability/termination rules, include valid repeatable branching and useful behavior evidence, report both goal pools and horizons, and provide an inconclusive outcome. Poor demo-pose CR alone must not reject the full framework.
- **[MAJOR] F4 — Restore theoretical premises throughout.** Replace all-z reuse and automatic capability-growth claims with sampled-update claims; distinguish learned from ideal policies; retain occupancy and full-input premises for contact impossibility; qualify the alleged confinement of executed planner conditions.
- **[MAJOR] F5 — Reconcile the experiment sequence with the diagnosed risks.** State whether E1 tests only the existing configuration or the proposed revised framework. Preserve a capped diagnostic path for exploration failure and keep deferred reuse claims explicitly pending.
- **[MAJOR] F6 — Make comparisons interpretable.** Specify the temporal-abstraction null or narrow its claimed coverage; define demo information, tracking-baseline behavior, observation access, update budgets, and pilot selection. Resolve which arms are mandatory versus deferred.
- **[MAJOR] F7 — Supply a real bounded budget and launch gate.** Convert control steps to measured runtime/compute estimates, cap pilots and extensions, account for probes/evaluation/restarts, and decide how much can run before E0. Define vector-step versus transition units.
- **[MAJOR] F8 — Separate source provenance and the three data paths.** Mark owner-reported versus specified versus verified implementation facts. Explicitly prohibit treating offline motion frames as collected transitions. Identify the applicable sealed prerequisites without presenting their local checks as production validation.
- **[MAJOR] F9 — Correct diagnostic and deferred-study interpretations.** Define the Q target and drift metric, avoid equating nonsignificance with near neutrality, and either complete C's controls/caps or label it a deferred design outline rather than a preregistration.
- **[MINOR] F10 — Remove cross-file contradictions and strengthen traceability.** Unify the deadlock Boolean rule, D1 goal-pool definition, Q gate status, and E1 arm list. Add exact source/result references for new numerical corrections, including loss normalization, and a compact pointer list for retained review limitations.

Recommendation: revise-then-adjudicate.
