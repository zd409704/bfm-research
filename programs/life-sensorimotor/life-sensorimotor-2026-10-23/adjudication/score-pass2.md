## Recommendation

**Recommend Q**, as provisional only: it passes a spike plan and not a mainline lock until the amendments below are met.

Conditions attached to the recommendation:
1. File the S0a/S0b split as an explicit ladder amendment in `decision.md`. State that S0a-first is justified as an **acceptance gate**, not as necessity-ordered research.
2. Add a latency sweep across L* (about 2–20 ms), with off-grid onsets, sensor noise and actuator lag. Replace the vacuous oracle and on-grid resolution checks. Add at least one humanoid-plausible mode (LIP with a = ~3.3 s⁻¹ plus one fast joint-level mode) and mark the 250 Hz and 1 kHz targets as placeholders until a mode-specific L* exists.
3. Name an owner and an approval path for envelope definitions and versions. Declare whether S0a is scoped per support mode.
4. Tighten B-N2's "equivalent physical mechanism", or downgrade B-N2 to a design heuristic.
5. P's best content is carried forward, not discarded: the C2 information-ceiling argument, the realized-torque export idea and `ident_flag` go to a separate S2a change request. P's C7 test needs a rate-matched, torque-access-matched redesign first.

## Rationale (one paragraph)

Q is better under the pre-registered rubric, 37 to 29, mainly on necessity discipline, constraint fit, evidence demand, complexity cost and integrability. It makes a small, correct and clearly scoped Grade-1 claim. It does not let spike results promote Candidate claims, and it states honestly what its argument does not establish. Its spike is cheap and runnable, but it is weak as a test: it passes by construction, so under the method's "spike or it didn't happen" rule it is only a sanity witness until made discriminating. P is the more ambitious and informative design, with a real falsifier and useful S3/S4 hooks. However, its ordering depends on a confounded benchmark comparison (C7), it extends its passivity guarantee past what its cited theorem covers, its decisive spike has undefined verdict rules, and its SA-1 runtime claim is infeasible as written. Under Appendix B, those are the failures that count most. The scores for the other eight rows differ by at most one point each, and the two proposals tie on falsifiability, failure modes, latency and stackability. A pass by P on its present spike would not remove the necessity-promotion problem, whereas Q's deficits can be fixed by amendment.

## Strongest unresolved objection to the recommendation

Q's gate may be vacuous or circular for the real target. The envelope (disturbance set, tolerance, latency budget) is undefined for the target plant, and for a legged body it depends on support and contact mode. So S0a either passes trivially on existing kHz joint firmware, giving the first mainline packet almost no research information, or it needs contact-mode information that it explicitly disclaims, which is circular. Q's own deadline fixture (a = 10 s⁻¹) is also unrepresentative of humanoid balance. If this objection holds, Q is a well-posed acceptance test of nothing important, and P's contact-observability concern (what the body must export upward) is the one that actually gates S2–S5.

## Spike outcome that would falsify the recommended proposal

Run the amended spike on a humanoid-plausible mode set. Q's S0a-first lock is falsified if either of the following occurs.
- **Latency sweep result:** with a = ~3.3 s⁻¹ and a stated push set and tolerance, the computed L* comfortably exceeds the ~20 ms policy-loop latency, so a 50 Hz loop already holds the envelope. No fast-tier deadline then comes out of the plant analysis. Tolerance to disturbances would then be governed by contact and support events (the S1/S2 problem), not by body-loop response, and S0a would be demoted to a trivial pass-through.
- **Per-support-mode result:** the certified envelope changes across single/double stance such that gait transitions necessarily violate the no-enlargement rule, or certification requires contact state.

Either result sends P1 back to the alternate (contact-observability-first, a redesigned P) via a brief amendment. Neither result alone shows the current Q harness is wrong. They show that the scalar fixture does not transfer.
