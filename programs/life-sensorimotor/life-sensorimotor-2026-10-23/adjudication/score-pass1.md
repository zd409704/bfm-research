## Recommendation

**Recommend Q** (provisional, with mandatory amendments before lock; see Rationale).

## Rationale (one paragraph)

On the fixed rubric Q scores higher (34 vs 30). It wins on constraint fit, evidence demand, complexity, reversibility and necessity discipline, and both red-team notes agree on that ordering. P is the richer and more consequential design, and its SA-2 has the only spike in the pair that can actually fail on a program-relevant question (PF-2). But as written it hides a confounded comparison, an infeasible runtime budget, a passivity guarantee beyond its cited theorem, and a lock rationale that leans on a benchmark-derived claim (C7), which Appendix B forbids. Q's mathematics is correct, its constraint-compliant plan is runnable verbatim, and it is cheap to abandon. Q's weakness is that it is close to uninformative: its spike passes by construction, and its gate may be vacuous or circular for a legged body. The recommendation is therefore conditional. The decision is provisional, not locked, and `decision.md` must (a) record S0a-first as an acceptance gate and not a necessity ordering, (b) file an explicit ladder amendment for the S0a/S0b split, (c) require a discriminating spike (an effective-latency sweep across L* for 2–20 ms, off-grid onsets, noise and actuator lag, with the vacuous checks removed), (d) re-derive the deadline for a CoM-height LIP and one joint-level mode and mark 250 Hz/1 kHz as placeholders until then, (e) name an envelope owner, and (f) handle contact-mode dependence. P's SA-2 (repaired to separate rate from torque access) is the natural next packet, S2a, once S0a is certified. This is a rubric-score recommendation, not a unanimous one: the human lead may reasonably prefer P if they weight program-relevant falsifiability above cost.

## Strongest unresolved objection to the recommendation

Q's gate may carry no research information. A scalar fixture with a = 10 s⁻¹, an arbitrary envelope, and a spike that passes by construction does not show that S0a is the right first lock for a legged humanoid. If target-plant firmware already runs kHz joint PD, S0a is "certify and reuse" with nothing learned. If the envelope varies with support mode, S0a needs the contact-mode distinguishability it claims to defer (S2), so it is circular, or it blocks S1 transitions through the no-enlargement rule. The recommendation does not resolve this. It only requires that the amended spike and the envelope definition confront it before lock.

## Spike outcome that would falsify the recommended proposal

Any of the following, on the amended spike, falsifies Q as the recommended first lock:
1. In the effective-latency sweep across L* (2–20 ms) for the indistinguishable pair, a controller with effective latency above L* = 9.53 ms keeps both worlds inside the envelope, or a controller well below L* (for example ≤ 5 ms) fails it under the pre-registered noise and actuator-lag settings. That would show B-N1 or its mapping to the harness is wrong.
2. For a CoM-height LIP (a ≈ 3.3 s⁻¹) with a stated push set and tolerance, the derived L* is compatible with a 50 Hz policy with no faster body loop, and no joint-level mode demands more. S0a's 250 Hz/1 kHz tier then has no necessity basis, and the gate reduces to a vacuous certification, so P/S2a-first or S1-first should be re-adjudicated.
3. The scoped envelope must be re-certified at each support configuration (single versus double stance) using contact-state information, which shows S0a cannot be separated from S2.
The existing run.py PASS alone does not count as a spike result for either direction.
