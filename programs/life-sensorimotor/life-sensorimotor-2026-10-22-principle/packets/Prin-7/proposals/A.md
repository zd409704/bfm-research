## 1. Thesis

The Grade-1 target in `claim.md` is easy to state and easy to make trivially true. Two facts make that so:

1. **Channel coincidence.** At the joint level, `τ_act_unmodeled` and `J_cᵀF` enter the dynamics **additively through the same input channel**. From `(q, q̇, τ_cmd)` and the nominal model, one can recover at most the **total residual** `r = τ_u + J_cᵀF` (Lemma L0, §4.1), up to sampling. Any identifiability of the contact therefore comes **only from prior structure that separates the nuisance class from the contact class**. It does not come from system structure.
2. **So the theorem is only as good as its model class.** If the nuisance class `U` is chosen large enough, the impossibility half follows in one line: nature can always produce a `u'` that equals `u + c`. That is a built-in conclusion of the H3 kind, and it is the formal version of the Prin-6 P-1 (iv) vacuity flag.

Hence the PLAN puts most of its weight on **admitting the model class**, not on the proof. A model class must be frozen before any proof or refuter work. Five properties make it admissible (§3):

- it is **physically sourced**: each nuisance family names a mechanism, and its envelope is set without reference to the contact class;
- it **declares sensor placement**: `U = U↑ ⊕ U↓` relative to the effort measurement, and a downstream-of-sensing variant is mandatory (H3);
- it has **witness realizability**: every confusing nuisance pair must be generated by the declared mechanism under a consistent hidden-state trajectory;
- it is **two-sided**: the upgrade target is a *separation* statement. Detection at the task spec must be **impossible** from `(q, q̇, τ_cmd)` history, **and** **possible** once the effort measurement `m = τ_cmd + τ_u↑` is added. Without the second half, `m` is not what makes the difference, and the result is a different claim (proprioceptive impossibility), not Factor E;
- it keeps **full class coverage**: every registered class with `d ≥ 10 ms` is retained. Holding only on a subclass is reported as such and routed as a proposed narrowing.

The **refuter** (§6) is a cross-family adversarial battery. Its task is to produce a **separating detector**: a function of permitted `(q, q̇, τ_cmd)` history that meets the spec over all of the frozen class. Or it shows the class fails admissibility. Each refuter line is predeclared against a specific structural escape route: the per-joint (diagonal) structure of friction versus the kinematic pattern of `J_cᵀF`; state-repeat consistency; onset-rate limits; adaptive identification; placement; sampling.

The attempt can succeed, fail, or stay unresolved. In no case does this packet, or the future execution packet, move the grade. Any proof goes through the B.9.2 Grade-1 check routes, then a separate grade-reassessment packet, then the human signature.

## 2. Formal setting (to be frozen as schema `K`)

A model class instance is a tuple

`K = (P, S, Π, U↑, U↓, C, Spec, Q)`

| Field | Content | Source premise |
|---|---|---|
| `P` | Plant: `n`-DOF rigid chain, known kinematics, nominal `M(q)`, `h(q, q̇)`. Actuation pattern declared: all joints actuated, or a declared under-actuated subset. Allowed inertial-model error, if any, is folded into `U` with its own envelope. | premise 1 |
| `S` | Sensing: `(q, q̇)` sampled at period `T`. Noise model `N`, either the noiseless strong form or a declared stochastic form. Quantization declared. | statement; H5 |
| `Π` | Permitted signal set for the detector: `(q, q̇)` samples, `τ_cmd` (commanded setpoints and the nominal control law), and command history **only as declared** under premise 6 / the C-P1-011 access-boundary record (Prin-6 P-1 (ii)). Variant `Π+m` adds `m = τ_cmd + τ_u↑` sampled at period `T_m`, with its own noise. | premises 5, 6 |
| `U↑` | Nuisance effort **upstream** of (contained in) the effort measurement: for example motor-side friction, or command-to-current saturation. | premise 2, H3 |
| `U↓` | Nuisance effort **downstream** of (not contained in) the effort measurement: for example joint-side friction past the transmission, transmission losses after the sensing point, backlash. **May not be empty in at least one registered variant.** | premise 2, H3 |
| `C` | Contact class: duration `d` (all registered classes with `d ≥ 10 ms`), magnitude band `[F_min, F_max]`, location set on non-foot links, onset profile family. | premise 3; scope |
| `Spec` | Detection specification: TPR `β` at a fixed false-event rate `α` (per second), plus a latency percentile bound `L_p`. These are **declared task values or symbolic parameters**. They are never spike thresholds (H5). Placeholder values limit only target-scope inferences (Prin-6 P-8). | premise 3 |
| `Q` | Quantifier mode: **worst-case** (nature chooses `u ∈ U` adversarially within the envelope; this is the primary mode) or **Bayesian** (prior on mechanism parameters; secondary mode). Declared before any proof. | — |

**Nuisance family schema.** Each of `U↑` and `U↓` is a sum of declared families `U_k`. Each family is a hidden-state generator

`ż_k = a_k(z_k, x, τ_cmd; θ_k)`, `τ_{u,k} = b_k(z_k, x, τ_cmd; θ_k)`, `θ_k ∈ Θ_k`, `z_k(0) ∈ Z_k`.

`x = (q, q̇)` is the state. The family is admitted only with the following stated: the mechanism name; the parameter box `Θ_k`; the magnitude envelope; the bandwidth or rate-of-change bound; the coupling structure across joints (diagonal or coupled); and the source of each bound (physical reasoning or a cited range). The candidate families below are **examples for the future packet to choose among**. They are not a choice made here:

- **F-static:** memoryless static friction `b(q̇)` (Coulomb plus viscous plus Stribeck, unknown shape inside an envelope). Per-joint diagonal.
- **F-hyst:** presliding or hysteretic friction with internal state `z` (bounded rate, per-joint).
- **F-drift:** slow parameter drift (thermal or wear), with a bounded rate `|θ̇| ≤ ρ`.
- **F-pos:** position-periodic ripple (cogging-like), with `θ`-indexed amplitude and known period class.
- **F-sat:** command-dependent saturation and gain error `b(τ_cmd)`.
- **F-trans:** transmission loss, load-dependent, downstream.
- **F-inert:** bounded inertial-model error, `ΔM q̈ + Δh`.

## 4. Theorem targets (statements to attempt; none argued here)

### 4.1 Lemma L0 — channel coincidence (expected short; still must be checked)

Under `P`, with exact nominal model, `(q, q̇)` known in continuous time, and `τ_cmd` known, the map from `(τ_u, c)` to observations factors through `r = τ_u + J_cᵀF`. Two pairs with equal `r` on `[0, t]` produce identical observations on `[0, t]`. Under sampling at period `T`, the observations factor further, through **per-interval linear functionals** of `r` (for the locally linearized double integrator, two per joint per interval: `∫r` and `∫(T − s) r ds`).

*Check route:* this is a standard unknown-input observability fact: two unknown inputs entering through the same input matrix are not separable by any output map. The B.9.2 check is a premise-by-premise match to an established system-theory result on left invertibility and unknown-input observability. The match must cover the nonlinear `M(q)` and the sampling step explicitly.

### 4.2 Theorem target T-E (Factor E, two-sided separation)

Fix an admissible `K`. Define the **confusion set**

`Conf(V) = { c ∈ C : ∃ realizable witness (AD-3) with u, u' ∈ V, u' = u + c on the decision window }`.

Target:

- **(T-E.i) Impossibility under `Π`.** If `Conf(U↑ ⊕ U↓)` covers a set of contacts large enough that `Spec` (β at α, `L_p`) cannot hold on some registered class, then no detector on `Π` meets `Spec` on that class. In worst-case mode, the observation laws under "contact `c`" and "no contact" coincide on `Conf`, so power ≤ size there. In Bayesian mode, the bound is quantitative through total-variation / two-point arguments.
- **(T-E.ii) Possibility under `Π+m`.** On the same class, `Conf(U↓)` is small enough that **some** detector on `Π+m` meets `Spec`. This must be shown constructively, by an explicit detector and a proof. A spike is not acceptable (B.3.5).
- **(T-E.iii) Envelope condition.** An explicit, checkable condition on the `K` envelopes, in terms of per-joint nuisance envelopes versus the per-joint contact torque pattern `J_cᵀF` and onset rates, under which T-E.i and T-E.ii hold. **This condition is the actual content of the upgrade.** T-E.i alone is near-trivial once overlap is granted.

Without T-E.ii or T-E.iii, the attempt has not produced a Grade-1 Factor-E statement (§7).

### 4.3 Theorem target T-R (Factor R, rate floor; secondary)

From L0's sampled form: at period `T`, contacts are distinguishable from nuisance only through per-interval functionals. Targets:

- **(T-R.i)** a latency floor for any detector on period-`T` data, as a function of `T`, `d` and the onset family;
- **(T-R.ii)** a confusion statement for `d ≲ T`: two residuals with equal per-interval functionals are indistinguishable. Overlap is then decided by whether `U` admits per-interval impulse pairs that match the contact class.

T-R is attempted only after T-E. It must also be two-sided: a floor at `T₂` that does not bind at `T₁`. Holding gain·T fixed (H2) is irrelevant to T-R, because T-R quantifies over all detectors.

### 4.4 Explicitly not targeted

- Grade-2 role necessity across implementation classes (that is the B.9.2 Grade-2 route, a different artifact).
- Foot and stance contacts; contact semantics; plants with dedicated force or tactile sensing (claim scope).
- Any statement about what a pretrained behavior model estimates.

3. **Order.** L0 → T-E on K-1J and K-1J↓ → refuter round 1 → T-E on K-2L and K-2L↓ → refuter round 2 → K-nD (only if the earlier rounds leave a non-empty surviving statement) → T-R.

5. **Allowed tools.** Symbolic derivation; formal-checker encoding of L0 and of T-E on the 1-DOF variants where feasible. No closed-loop sim spike, no factorial run, and no target-plant measurement belong to this attempt.
6. **Budget and stop rules** (the future packet fixes the numbers): a bounded number of prover rounds per variant; a bounded number of refuter rounds; stop on (a) a refuter witness admitted by audit, (b) a complete two-sided proof per variant, or (c) budget exhaustion, which yields **unresolved** (Prin-6 P-5).

## 6. Refuter battery (predeclared lines of attack)

The refuter succeeds against a variant by delivering **either** a separating detector on `Π` with a proof that it meets `Spec` over the whole frozen variant, **or** an admissibility failure of the variant (an AD-item violated). Each line is predeclared so that a later `K` edit cannot dodge it silently.

| ID | Line | Escape route it tests | Expected status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **RF-1 Cross-joint structure** | Per-joint (diagonal) nuisance versus the kinematic pattern of `J_cᵀF`. A contact on link `k` produces correlated torques across joints `1..k` in a geometry-determined direction. With diagonal `U`, overlap requires every per-joint component to fit in that joint's envelope **at the same time**, with consistent hidden states. | The product-set overlap may be far smaller than the per-joint envelope suggests. Strong on K-2L and K-nD. | Live threat |
| **RF-2 State-repeat consistency** | If `U` is memoryless in `(x, τ_cmd)`, or drifts slowly (`ρ` small relative to `d`), then revisits to the same `(x, τ_cmd)` pin `τ_u`, and a contact shows up as an inconsistency. | F-static and F-drift alone may make contacts identifiable. | **RF-3 Onset rate** | A rate-of-change bound on `U` versus contact onset profiles. A contact whose onset is faster than any admissible `u̇` separates. | Forces `C`'s onset family and `U`'s rate bounds to be stated honestly. Interacts with T-R. | Live threat |
| **RF-4 Adaptive identification** | Online identification of `θ` under persistent excitation shrinks the effective envelope over time, leaving only the non-identifiable residual. | Impossibility must hold against learning detectors (AD-5). | **RF-5 Placement** | In ↓ variants, show that `Conf(U↓) ≈ Conf(U↑ ⊕ U↓)` on some registered class, so `m` does not help. | Attacks T-E.ii. ## 8. Relation to the vacuity flag (Prin-6 P-1 (iv))

Two-sidedness (AD-4) gives a **non-vacuous, implementation-independent information criterion** for Factor E. A measurement realizes the Factor-E content **iff** it shrinks `Conf` on in-class contacts, from `Conf(U↑ ⊕ U↓)` to `Conf(U↓)`. That is narrower and testable on paper. The plan **does not** propose importing this criterion into the Grade-2 role text. Such an operationalization would restrict which channels count as realizations, and that is an AM to be counted (Prin-6 follow-up 2). It is offered only as an input to the B.9.2 Grade-2 argument that the ruling says must address P-1 (iv).

## 12. Known weaknesses / objections invited

1. **Worst-case mode may be physically pessimistic.** A worst-case impossibility can hold while typical-case detection succeeds. Mitigation: worst case is the declared primary mode, and the result is labeled as such. A Bayesian-mode refuter success is filed as AN on relevance. It does not falsify the worst-case statement.
2. **Envelope sourcing is a judgment call** (AD-1). Mitigation: RF-6 audit by the cross-family refuter, plus freezing. The residual risk is that both families accept the same implausible range.
3. **The K-nD target may be out of reach.** Mitigation: O-3 routing. Low-dimensional results are reported as subclass results, not as the claim.
4. **AD-7 pre-ruling may consume the B.9.6 budget** before any evidence arrives. This is intentional: it makes the cost of regularity premises visible up front.
5. **Hidden-state witnesses (F-hyst) may carry the whole Impossibility half.** If so, the honest statement is "Factor E is necessary iff the target plant has hysteretic or otherwise non-repeatable nuisance above envelope X". That is a premise-match question for the target (Prin-6 P-9 premise-failure route), not something settled here.
