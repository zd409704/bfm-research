## Main findings and required revisions

### 1. The lock path is broadly sound but is not yet a complete signing procedure

C2 correctly excludes model re-derivation alone as the required Grade-1 verification, and correctly offers the three B.9.2 routes. C4–C6 also protect the central distinction: a necessary latency bound is not a sufficient invariance certificate, and measured response delay is not automatically a common-input interval. A is right that a failed counterexample search is not an additional mandatory Grade-1 lock condition.

The published-result option is underspecified. Matching premises to a variation-of-constants formula checks the solution identity; it does not by itself check the entire claimed impossibility result. The verification artifact must cover the indistinguishability/common-input premise, paired disturbance construction, triangle inequality, horizon, and final quantifiers as well. Either identify an established result covering that conclusion and match its premises, or independently verify the residual argument through an allowed route. The optional artifact hash is useful provenance, not evidence of correctness.

Calling mechanization the “cheapest” route is unsupported. A real-analysis development with Carathéodory solutions and locally integrable forcing could cost more than qualified human review. Describe it as an option with an unestimated cost; choose the route after checking available infrastructure. The minimal sequence should also retain route (b) if it genuinely satisfies B.9.2, rather than silently dropping it.

C3 is not safely classifiable in advance as “precision only.” Reinterpreting every control path, changing a horizon requirement, or clarifying randomization can alter scope or quantifiers. Compare each proposed confirmation with the current claim; record any substantive premise addition under B.9.6 and make verification apply to the exact final statement. Passive stabilization may indeed change the modeled dynamics, but that is a premise-match question, not a universal exemption asserted by wording alone.

Finally, human signature is necessary but does not replace B.9.1. State the closing sequence explicitly: resolve the record and scope/count questions; verify the final statement; assemble the complete scope-limited package; obtain the human signature; have the bound, non-author claim adjudicator record the transition. A's statement that the claim remains Candidate until signature is correct as a necessary condition, but signature must not appear sufficient by itself. Verify the claimed author/adjudicator binding against the governing record before execution.

### 2. The inventory supports conservative deferral, not a finding that no trigger has fired

A discloses that it did not read the upgrade/violation histories or most claim records. That is good provenance, but it conflicts with the categorical thesis “No Candidate has met a retire trigger,” the description of remaining work as merely administrative, and the row saying C-P1-001 is “not narrowed.” C9 itself remains unconfirmed.

Use the narrower disposition: **no retirement nomination is justified by the materials inspected; confirm histories before deciding whether mandatory review is due.** At the third scope-narrowing event B.9.6 requires retirement review, not automatic retirement. A should make that consequence explicit. The proposal's reported narrowing count for C-P1-012 and absence of violations elsewhere should remain attributed, pending record verification.

Retiring a Grade-3 note also need not mean its historical content was refuted. Leaving it quarantined is a reasonable low-cost choice, but the justification should be its continuing research utility and lack of a demonstrated retirement reason, not that retirement necessarily asserts falsity.

### 3. The priority list is valid planning; its proposed mathematical upgrade needs sharper premises

The ordering is defensible: audit the Eng-2 ceiling lemma, finish a small verification package, then investigate structural identifiability. It neither proves a ladder edge nor grants a Candidate veto. Preserve that separation when assigning work: an unchecked Candidate lemma can motivate an audit, and a demonstrated mathematical defect can undermine an engineering inference, without making successful adjudication a new Principle gate for Eng-2.

The rank-3 statement that time/frequency overlap makes contact effort unidentifiable is a research target, not yet a theorem. Overlap alone does not establish indistinguishability; known dynamics, nuisance restrictions, or informative interventions may distinguish the terms. Require an explicit admissible model class and two different contact/nuisance decompositions producing identical allowed observations under the same protocol. Also name what would refute the proposed impossibility within that class. File any actual counterexample search under a predeclared PLAN (B.9.8).

The phrase “grade stays 2 until B.9.2 is met” conflates grade with lock state. B.9.2 governs locking. An adjudicator may assess a proposed Grade-1 argument while the claim remains Candidate; human signature is still required for a lock. Distinguish a proposed upgrade, an adjudicated grade, and a Locked state.

“Leave until Eng-2 data” should likewise mean no present state-change nomination, not a ban on the independent rank-3 proof work. Eng-2 data can expose premise failures or motivate challenges; a PASS alone cannot promote necessity. The claim that ranks 1–3 have different adjudicators is not established by A's cited bindings, so parallel scheduling should depend on actual ownership and capacity.
