# Blind Review A on Proposal B (Packet B01)

**Reviewer:** Blind Reviewer A. **Inputs read:** `PROGRAM_CHARTER.md`, `packets/B01/brief.md`, `packets/B01/proposals/B.md`. To check the proposal's factual claims, I also read `baseline/02` §p_5065 (lines 40–46), `baseline/03` Audit Issue #4, and the pinned method's §11.5 and Appendix B. I read no other file in `reviews/` or `drafts/`.

**Summary verdict:** Eligible (no hard-constraint violation). The proposal is careful about causality and honest about its scope. Its central proof covers the *weaker* case (non-identifiability on a deterministic manifold). It leaves out the stronger result that a population-optimal critic conditioned on a stochastic endogenous window has zero action derivative, and that stronger result is the one most likely to apply to V3. Replacing all of Groups 18–21 risks throwing away current physical state that baseline/03 places inside that block. The spike has no arm that separates *endogenous* leakage from *exogenous* preview, so it cannot test the boundary the brief is about.

## 2. Rubric-Keyed Technical Objections

### 2.1 Action-Jacobian Degeneracy Proof (35%)

- **[MAJOR] The proof covers the non-identified case but not the determinate-degeneracy case that likely applies to V3.** The `F_c` family (§2.2) is correct. On a deterministic manifold `l = b + a`, values do not determine the partial derivative. But the learned `c` is then set by inductive bias, so the result does not show that leakage *does* bypass `a_t`, only that it *can*. The brief asks for a proof of how leakage bypasses the action input. The stronger, determinate statement is short. Suppose the window carries innovation: `L = h(S,a) + η`, with `η` not determined by `(S,a)` (stochastic generators, contact noise, or for `K>1` the stochastic behaviour continuation `a_{t+1:t+K-1}` under the 1/3-rule / 2/3-policy mixture). Suppose also that `L` screens off the action, `Y ⊥ a | (S, L)`. Then the MSE population minimizer is `E[Y|S,a,L] = f(S,L)`, and `∂_a` of it is **identically zero on the support**. The zero gradient is then the *unique* optimum, not one admissible extension among many. Under partial screening, `J_a` reduces to the residual direct effect. Proposal B notes that noise breaks collinearity (§2.1 iterated-expectation remark), but it never states this consequence. Without it, the 35% row rests on the weaker of the two available theorems.
- **[MAJOR] Policy mismatch in the decomposition `J_phys = J_a + J_L H_a` for `K>1`.** §2.3 fixes "future policy/noise convention", but RAW windows in replay were generated by the behaviour mixture `μ`. In the spike (§5.3), `H_a` is taken from simulator branches "with the same future policy", which is unspecified and presumably the fixed evaluation `π`. When `μ ≠ π`, `J_L H_a` mixes a `μ`-learned `J_L` with a `π`-generated `H_a`, and the omitted-sensitivity identity no longer holds. The proposal needs to name which policy generates `a_{t+1:t+K-1}` in both training data and branches, or restrict the bypass gate to `K=1` slices.
- **[MINOR] Arithmetic.** `ε/(1−ε)` at `ε = 0.05` is `0.05263… = 5.26%`, not `5.27%` (§2.3).
- **[MINOR]** The score-function term `E[F̃ ∂_a log p(L|S,a)]` (§2.1) needs `L` to have an action-dependent density. That fails for the deterministic `h` used in §2.2 and at contact switches, where `p(L|S,a)` has atoms. The text mentions a reparameterization alternative. State which applies at stop engagement (E1), where `[·]_+` makes `h` piecewise and the law of `L` mixed.

### 2.2 Completeness of 22-Group Audit (25%)

- **[MAJOR] Replacing the whole 18–21 block probably deletes current physical state.** `baseline/03` Audit Issue #4 says Groups 18–21 pack future `τ_ext,t+1:t+K` *"alongside current solver states"*. The proposal replaces all 726 dims with `E_726`, and the scalar template (§1.3) carries only generator quantities (commanded torque, phase, countdowns, flags). If current solver, contact, or applied-load states live in 18–21 and are not duplicated in 1–17, they are lost. That is a disambiguation regression, and the brief explicitly asks for slice-level treatment ("which exact slices"). The proposal should carry over admissible *current* slices of 18–21 unchanged (R), mask or replace only the endogenous future slices, and check for redundancy against 13–17 rather than assume it.
- **[MAJOR] "Mask" is the default for acausal slices outside 18–21, which is not a causal replacement.** The rubric asks for "the causal replacement for every acausal slice". For Groups 4, 5, 14 and 16 (actuator/constraint/contact forces from the solve that produces `s_{t+1}`), the proposal masks. The obvious causal replacement is the **lag-1 completed-interval value**, recollected and not timestamp-shifted, which keeps E1/E2 contact-force observability. §1.2 forbids shifting stale records (correct), but it never specifies lagged recollection as the replacement. So masking silently removes the same contact-force information that §1.4's E1/E2 table relies on.
- **[MINOR] The exogenous future preview is discarded without justification.** By the brief's own definition, `τ_ext,t+1:t+K` from a pre-sampled open-loop schedule is exogenous Markov state (`H_a = 0`), and §1.3 concedes it is legal. Compressing it into a hand-built generator template (`[8:40)`) only works for the scalar-torque family. The proposal itself says it does not establish sufficiency for multi-event, vector or feedback generators. A retained world-frame exogenous preview generalizes to any open-loop generator with no per-family encoding, and should be the default with the template as an option. See also §3.
- **[MINOR] The provenance of the regime one-hot is unspecified.** Slot `[0:5)` is "current E0–E4 one-hot". If step-level E3/E4 labels are assigned retrospectively (for example, E4 marked once recovery is detected; compare Audit Issue #1), the label is itself acausal. Require it to be derived only from generator state at `t^-`.

### 2.3 Preservation of E0–E4 Disambiguation (20%)

- **Strength:** §1.1 separates causal availability from Markov sufficiency. The §3.2 / C-B01-B-02 construction correctly shows why history-only (Group 22) aliasing fails for hidden pre-sampled release times. Threshold-triggered release is handled correctly as reactive state, not as a countdown.
- **[MAJOR]** The two objections in §2.2 (the wholesale 18–21 deletion and masking contact forces in 1–6 / 13–17) are disambiguation risks. The E1/E2 rows of the §1.4 table cite "current/past contact … state" and "current margins/penetration", but the construction gives no guarantee they survive. Add a pre-training check: for every retained-or-masked decision, fit a probe from `(x_t, p_t^causal)` to the original *current* slice and require recovery (for example `R² ≥ 0.99`). Otherwise disambiguation is asserted, not checked.
- **[MINOR]** E2 obstacle/external-body generators (moving obstacles, if any) are not covered by the template. `[40:64)` is "zero for scalar template", so for those the E2 Markov claim depends on an unwritten encoding.

### 2.4 Spike Falsifiability (20%)

The protocol is unusually complete: frozen seeds, no checkpoint selection, independent physical references, a branch-based `D_ref`, and underpowered-test rules. Specific defects are in §4.

## 3. Audit of Rejected Alternative & Necessity Claim

### 3.1 Rejected alternatives (method §11.5)

- **§3.1 "detach the future window" is close to a strawman.** The proposal itself says replay tensors "ordinarily already have no autograd link". DETACH is therefore the status quo under another name, and the `c = 1` example dismisses it immediately. It meets the letter of §11.5 but adds little evidential weight.
- **§3.2 "EMA-only (mask 18–21)" is a genuine alternative,** rejected with a valid information lower bound (C-B01-B-02), and the scope is stated honestly (it is sufficient when generator state is determined by history).
- **Stronger alternatives that were not seriously evaluated:**
  1. **Retain only the exogenous preview** (§2.2 above). This is the brief's own admissible class, and it is simpler and more general than the template.
  2. **A causal learned imputer `L̂ = ĥ(S_t, a_t)`** fed to `F`, which keeps the `J_L Ĥ_a` route differentiable without simulator differentiation. §3.1 mentions full rollout recomputation and defers it, but the cheap learned-imputer version is a direct competitor to "replace with generator state" for endogenous slices and should be argued against explicitly.

### 3.2 C-B01-B-01 against Appendix B

- **B.3(1) Explicit premises:** present. **B.3(2) Conclusion shape:** impossibility (non-identifiability), which is admissible. **B.3(3) Implementation independence:** satisfied; the construction is function-class level.
- **[MAJOR] Premise (iv) contains the conclusion, so the falsifier is vacuous.** Premise (iv) assumes "two differentiable extensions that agree on the manifold but differ in their partial action derivative". The conclusion, that values cannot uniquely identify the partial derivative, is a restatement of (iv). The "more general" falsifier ("demonstrate unique identification … without removing admissible extensions") is therefore impossible by definition. The only live falsifier is an algebra error in `F_c`, which is trivially checkable. As written, the claim is analytically true and carries almost no information about V3. B.3(4) is met only nominally.
- **[MAJOR] Applicability to V3 is doubtful and has no empirical test.** The real content sits in premises (ii) and (iii): a deterministic `L = h(S,a)` and critic values constrained only on that manifold. For V3 (stochastic generators, `K>1` windows driven by the stochastic `μ` continuation, contact noise), `Var(L | S, a) > 0` is likely. In that case the data are *not* confined to the manifold, non-identifiability fails, and the determinate zero-gradient result from §2.1 applies instead. The operational test (`c ∈ {0,1,2}` in FP64) checks only the construction, not the premises. Add a premise check: use the restored-snapshot branches already in Stage B to estimate `Var(L | S, a)` per slice. Then classify each endogenous slice as "manifold" (C-01 applies) or "stochastic" (the screening result applies). The claim, or a sibling claim, should then cover the stochastic case as the main Grade-1 statement for this packet.
- **Grade/State discipline:** correct. It is Candidate, a passing spike explicitly cannot promote it, and there is no veto. This fully complies with B.4/B.9.

## 4. Spike Protocol Critique

**Missing controls / arms**

1. **[MAJOR] No EXO-PREVIEW arm.** RAW vs CAUSAL changes two things at once: it removes endogenous reactions *and* exogenous previews, and it swaps raw fields for a template. An arm with endogenous future slices masked and the exogenous `τ_ext` preview retained is the only way to test the brief's core exogenous/endogenous boundary. It also tests whether the template costs information (H1).
2. **[MAJOR] No information-vs-distribution control.** Add RAW-PERMUTED: shuffle the 18–21 future slices across samples within each cell, keeping marginals and normalizer statistics and destroying information. Without it, a CAUSAL-vs-RAW difference in `J_a` error could come from input dimensionality, scale, or normalizer differences (RAW future-field scales are fitted separately, §5.2) rather than from the information shortcut.
3. **[MINOR] No EMA-ONLY arm (mask 18–21).** This is the empirical side of the §3.2 rejection and of C-B01-B-02's operational test, but it is absent from the arm list.
4. **[MINOR] DETACH is redundant as a trained arm.** Identical input values give identical parameter gradients, so under the declared deterministic paired runs DETACH must be *bitwise* identical to RAW. The `≤ 5%` median-error tolerance is far too loose for a negative control. Require exact equality and drop the extra training compute.

**Unmeasured confounds**

5. **[MAJOR] Horizon/termination mismatch between critic and reference.** Stage B episodes are 64 steps, but `D_ref` and `F̂_ref` use `H = 200` continuations. The proposal does not say whether episode end is treated as terminal (`d = 1`) or as time-limit truncation with a bootstrap. If it is terminal, the critic learns a truncated value and the derivative-recovery gate fails systematically regardless of causality. The proposal must also define the generator schedule beyond step 64 for continuations.
6. **[MAJOR] Underfitting is confounded with causality.** `2,000 × 128 = 256k` draws is about 1.4 passes over 184,320 transitions for 5,065-D twin critics. "Action derivative recovery" (median relative error ≤ 0.20) then mixes fit quality with causal correctness. RAW may also fit faster *because* it has the shortcut. Report a fit-matched comparison, for example compare `J_a` error at matched held-out factual NRBR, or add a capacity/updates sweep labelled as non-gating.
7. **[MINOR] Behaviour-policy mismatch in `H_a`** (see §2.1). The continuation policy for branch windows must match the one that generated the RAW training windows. Otherwise the RAW-bypass gate's `‖J_L H_a‖` is not the omitted term.

**Threshold flaws**

8. **[MINOR] The counterfactual-invariance tolerance is wrong in kind.** For identical snapshots, packing should be **bitwise** identical. An absolute FP32 tolerance of `1e-7` is too tight for recomputed values with magnitude ≫ 1 (FP32 ulp ≈ 1.2e-7 relative). It is also too loose for leaks that are weakly coupled to `a` over the ±0.01–0.02 branch grid, since post-normalization differences can fall below 1e-7. Use exact equality for the packing check and a *normalized* relative test for the output-invariance check.
9. **[MINOR] The forbidden-sensitivity gate is tautological by construction.** `sanitize` never reads `raw[idx_future]`, so `J_L ≡ 0` follows from the code. It checks the adapter, as the proposal concedes, and leaks outside 18–21 are invisible to it. Coverage elsewhere relies on the branch-invariance gate, so item 8 is the gate that matters.
10. **[MINOR] Seed-level inference with n = 5.** Paired resampling of five seeds yields very coarse 95% intervals. The `4/5`-seed vote rules are the meaningful criterion, so state that the seed-bootstrap intervals are descriptive.
11. **[MINOR] The Bellman-consistency comparator is weak.** "CAUSAL not more than 0.02 worse than RAW queried with fixed recorded windows under action interventions" compares against an arm expected to be poor on counterfactuals. The absolute `NRBR ≤ 0.15` gate carries the weight. Either drop the relative clause or compare against the EXO-PREVIEW arm (item 1).
12. **[MINOR] H1 target mismatch.** The charter's H1 is about `F`'s *TD target* EV, but §5.5 measures EV against an MC reference `Y_ref`. That is defensible, but declare it as a deviation from the charter's operationalization. Also, `EV_RAW` on the factual panel is inflated by realized innovations, which the proposal itself notes, so a failure of `EV_C/EV_R > 0.95` may reflect the unpredictability of the realized innovations rather than a defect of the repair. Pre-register how such a failure will be read (for example, comparing against EXO-PREVIEW).

**Priority fixes for the author:** (a) add the screening-off / zero-derivative theorem for stochastic windows and the `Var(L|S,a)` premise check; (b) audit 18–21 slice by slice (retain current slices; mask or lag only endogenous ones; consider an exogenous preview); (c) add EXO-PREVIEW and RAW-PERMUTED arms; (d) define termination and continuation policy for `D_ref`.
