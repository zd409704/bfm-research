## 1. Singular-Value Decomposition of the 144-Configuration Outcome Operator

### 1.1 Distinguish the three matrices being discussed

There are three different rank questions:

1. The singular spectrum of physical outcome samples, before encoding.
2. The spectrum of the learned 16-by-16 conditional embedding second moment.
3. The singular spectrum of the successor operator that the bilinear model must approximate.

A small intrinsic state dimension does not prove that a successor operator has rank at most 16. Conversely, having 144 configurations does not by itself require 144 independent right-side outcome features. Neither conclusion follows from counting configurations or input coordinates.

Write configuration as `k=(geometry_seed, stroke_profile)`, scene as `e`, capacity tier as `c(k)`, and replay partition as `r`. Let

\[
\alpha=(.20,.15,.20,.15,.30),\qquad
\beta_{\rm rule}=1/3,\quad\beta_{\rm policy}=2/3.
\]

For the rank diagnostic, define a fixed, broad reference distribution in each configuration,

\[
\rho_k=\sum_{e,r}\alpha_e\beta_r\rho_{k,e,r},\qquad
\rho_*={1\over144}\sum_k\rho_k.
\]

These distributions include static anchors and dynamic trajectories. They are not distributions restricted to one static target. The evaluation distribution is frozen independently of each arm's realized replay occupancy.

### 1.2 Exact length scaling of the physical outcome matrix

Let `omega = qdot`, `a = qddot_hat`, and let `u` denote the housing-frame unit rotation axis, `(0,0,1)`. For a rigid tip vector of length `L>0`, set `rhat(q)=p_tip(q)/L`. The canonical outcome is

\[
g_L=(\cos q,\sin q,\omega,a,
L\hat r,\ L\omega(u\times\hat r),\
L[a(u\times\hat r)+\omega^2u\times(u\times\hat r)]).
\]

For matched angular trajectories and matched normalized tip geometry, this is exactly

\[
g_L=D_Lh,\qquad D_L=\operatorname{diag}(I_4,L I_9).
\]

If `H` is a row matrix of matched canonical samples and `G_L=H D_L`, the squared singular values of the weighted outcome matrix are the eigenvalues of

\[
G_L^T W G_L=D_L C_h D_L
=\begin{pmatrix}C_{aa}&L C_{at}\\L C_{ta}&L^2C_{tt}\end{pmatrix},
\quad C_h=H^TWH.
\]

The same congruence holds for centered covariances when the same samples are centered first. Thus tip-block energy scales as `L^2` and angular-tip cross moments as `L`. Across the permitted length endpoints, the matched tip-energy ratio is `(0.45/0.08)^2 = 31.640625`. This is an exact scaling calculation, **not a measured dominance ratio in V3**. Different strokes, dynamics, initial conditions, and normalized tip geometry change `C_h`; they cannot be removed by this calculation. A raw SVD mixing meters, radians, and seconds also depends on the chosen units. All reported physical SVDs must therefore specify their scaling.

In the planar specialization `rhat=(cos q,sin q,0)`,

\[
p=L(\cos q,\sin q,0),\quad
v=L\omega(-\sin q,\cos q,0),
\]
\[
\hat a=L\{a(-\sin q,\cos q,0)-\omega^2(\cos q,\sin q,0)\}.
\]

Within a fixed configuration these 13 entries are generated by three dynamic variables `(q,omega,a)`. The sample matrix nevertheless contains nonlinear products such as `omega cos q` and `omega^2 sin q`. Its linear rank is not restricted to three. A nonlinear encoder can likewise have 16 nonzero second-moment eigenvalues on a continuous low-dimensional manifold. This possibility is not a guarantee of useful successor prediction.

The sphere projection also matters: `||B(g)||^2=16` makes every encoded observation contribute the same trace. Long links cannot dominate the *encoded trace* merely by having larger physical coordinates. They can still dominate feature learning through input conditioning, Jacobians, occupancy, and angular allocation on the sphere. This is the mechanism to test, rather than assuming unbounded covariance energy after sphere projection.

### 1.3 Define the common successor operator before invoking SVD

Fix a causal evaluation policy, discount, row sampling law, and common outcome reference measure. Rows contain configuration-conditioned source contexts and actions; columns are common outcome functions. A finite projection of the discounted successor operator is

\[
M_k(i,j)=(1-\gamma)\,\mathbb E\left[
\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^t\phi_j(g_{t+1})\mid k,s_i,a_i,\pi_{z_i}\right],
\]

where the `phi_j` are orthonormal under the same frozen outcome reference measure. The evaluation construction in Section 5 uses more than 16 columns and no learned encoder to define the target.

With row weights `W_k` summing to one within each configuration, stack

\[
\mathcal M=\begin{bmatrix}
\sqrt{1/144}\,W_1^{1/2}M_1\\\vdots\\
\sqrt{1/144}\,W_{144}^{1/2}M_{144}
\end{bmatrix}=U\operatorname{diag}(\sigma_j)V^T.
\]

The projected bilinear approximation has form `F_k A_B`, where

\[
A_B(:,j)=\mathbb E_{g\sim\rho_*}[B(g)\phi_j(g)],
\]

and the forward outputs are scaled consistently with the `(1-gamma)` convention. Every such stacked approximation has rank at most 16. Allowing an arbitrary rank-16 matrix gives the optimistic lower bound

\[
\epsilon_{16,*}^2=
{\sum_{j>16}\sigma_j^2\over\sum_j\sigma_j^2}.
\]

This is an exact finite-matrix rank bound. The sphere constraint, finite network capacity, and optimization can make the implemented model worse. A finite projected operator can also miss difficult functions in the full operator. Its small tail is evidence on this panel, not a proof for all possible rewards or goals.

The conditional matrices `M_k` can each have a small tail while their stack has a large tail: their useful right singular subspaces may differ. Conversely, if `M_k=A_k V^T` for a common outcome basis of width at most 16, the stack has rank at most 16 despite arbitrarily different left factors `A_k`. Conditioning `F` can represent these different left factors. It **cannot increase the dimension of the shared right span** or reconstruct distinctions already erased by `B`.

### 1.4 What the 0.943 objection actually establishes

The stated `sqrt(1-16/144)` lower bound is exact for 144 equal-energy, orthogonal **right-side outcome modes**, for example blocks

\[
M_k=a_k v_k^T,\qquad ||a_k||=||v_k||=1,\quad
v_k^Tv_l=\delta_{kl}.
\]

Their equally weighted stack has 144 identical nonzero squared singular values. It therefore has optimal relative rank-16 error

\[
\sqrt{1-16/144}=0.942809\ldots.
\]

Pairwise Frobenius orthogonality of *whole transition matrices* is a weaker condition and does not imply this spectrum. Counterexample: choose orthonormal `a_k` and one common unit vector `v`; then `M_k=a_k v^T` are mutually Frobenius-orthogonal, but their vertical stack has rank one. Vectorizing these matrices and taking the SVD of the vectorized collection would analyze a different bottleneck from the common right-side factorization `F B^T`.

Thus the baseline worst case is a valid warning under its stronger spectral premise, not a proved property of the 144 physical configurations. Housing-frame alignment and explicit length handling make a shared basis plausible; they do not establish the charter's `<0.20` error hypothesis. We must measure the stacked spectrum and the worst configuration's approximation error on a common reference.

### 1.5 Exact conditional effective rank, and the limit of what can be derived

Follow the baseline's uncentered convention:

\[
\Sigma_k=\mathbb E_{\rho_k}[b b^T],\quad b=B(g),
\qquad \mu_k=\mathbb E_{\rho_k}b,
\qquad C_k=\Sigma_k-\mu_k\mu_k^T.
\]

`Sigma_k` is a second moment, although the baseline calls it covariance. On the radius-four sphere, `tr(Sigma_k)=16`. This is the ideal mathematical projection contract. Audit any epsilon-stabilized implementation: normalize empirical eigenvalues by their actual trace, report norm deviations, and do not use the trace-16 certificate if that contract fails beyond `1e-6` relative tolerance. Zero encoder outputs are integrity failures. Let the ideal moment's eigenvalues be `lambda_ki`. Define

\[
p_{ki}=\lambda_{ki}/16,\qquad
\operatorname{erank}(\Sigma_k)=
\exp\left[-\sum_{i=1}^{16}p_{ki}\log p_{ki}\right],\quad0\log0=0.
\]

This is entropy effective rank applied to the PSD matrix, following [Roy and Vetterli, *The Effective Rank: A Measure of Effective Dimensionality* (2007)](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.40328). For samples with normalized weights `w_n`, form `Z_k[n,:]=sqrt(w_n)b_n^T`. If its singular values are `s_ki`, the exact empirical formula uses `lambda_ki=s_ki^2`. Using normalized `s_ki` instead would report effective rank of `Z_k`, a different statistic. We also report the centered version from `C_k`; zero centered trace is assigned centered effective rank zero.

There are no learned `B` weights or outcome distributions in the frozen packet from which numerical per-configuration ranks can be derived. Claiming such numbers for either training loss would fabricate results. The exact statements available before training are:

| Objective and attained condition | Exact implication for a configuration |
|---|---|
| Global: `sum_k pi_k Sigma_k = I` | No nontrivial universal guarantee beyond `1 <= erank(Sigma_k) <= 16`. |
| IPW pooled: `sum_k Sigma_k / 144 = I` | The same limitation, even with exact probabilities. |
| Conditional: every `Sigma_k = I` | Every `erank(Sigma_k) = 16`. Attainability is an additional assumption. |
| Conditional defect `delta_k^2 = ||Sigma_k-I||_F^2` | `erank(Sigma_k) >= 256 / (16 + delta_k^2)`. |

For the last row, spectral Shannon entropy is at least order-two Renyi entropy, so entropy effective rank is at least `1/sum_i p_ki^2`. Since `tr(Sigma_k^2)=16+delta_k^2`, the bound follows. In particular,

\[
\|\Sigma_k-I\|_F^2\le16/3
\ \Longrightarrow\ \operatorname{erank}(\Sigma_k)\ge12.
\]

This condition is sufficient, not necessary. The actual eigenvalues remain the gate statistic.

For an exact counterexample to pooled guarantees, assign nine of the 144 groups to each coordinate axis. Within each group let `b=+4e_j` or `-4e_j` equiprobably. Then `Sigma_k=16e_j e_j^T`, so every conditional rank is one, but the uniform pooled second moment is `I`. No batch size or accurate inverse-probability estimator repairs the missing conditional constraint in that example. Distinguishable group supports suffice to realize it; the example does not assert that physical V3 outcomes have those supports.

## 2. Stratified Orthonormalization & Nondimensionalization Proposal

### 2.1 (a) Global L_orth: useful control, inadequate certificate

The unweighted baseline estimator is

\[
\widehat\Sigma_q={1\over n}\sum_i b_i b_i^T,
\qquad L_{\rm global}={1\over16}\|\widehat\Sigma_q-I\|_F^2.
\]

It targets the realized batch law `q`, not the intended `rho_*`, if quotas drift. Even perfect optimization under the intended mixture permits the counterexample above. This arm is retained to quantify the combined consequences of quota drift and conditional collapse.

### 2.2 (b) Scene-and-capacity inverse-probability weighted L_orth

Let `N_c` be the manifest count of configurations in capacity tier `c`. For the desired distribution uniform over configurations, the desired scene-capacity mass is

\[
\nu(e,c)=\alpha_e N_c/144.
\]

If configurations within each capacity and rule/policy proportions are already sampled correctly, the exact correction is

\[
w_i={\nu(e_i,c_i)\over q(e_i,c_i)},\qquad
\widehat\Sigma_*={1\over n}\sum_i w_i b_i b_i^T.
\]

If partition probabilities also drift, use `nu(e,c,r)=alpha_e (N_c/144) beta_r`. If configuration selection within a tier is nonuniform, the scene-capacity correction alone is insufficient: use the actual hierarchical draw probability

\[
q(k,e,r)=q(e,c,r)\,q(k\mid e,c,r),\qquad
w_i={\alpha_{e_i}\beta_{r_i}/144\over q(k_i,e_i,r_i)}.
\]

All probabilities are sampler probabilities, including replacement and partition selection, not raw buffer occupancy estimates. In this proposal, sampling is uniform within each resident `(k,e,r)` cell; otherwise the within-cell selection correction must also appear. The numerator and denominator describe the same selected outcome convention.

No positive target mass may have zero sampling probability. Empty cells cause `INSUFFICIENT_SUPPORT`, not weight clipping or an invented covariance. The sampler uses a 10% mixture with direct target-law sampling to bound `w <= 10`; that mixture is included in `q`. Readiness still requires actual resident witnesses. Report effective sample size `(sum w)^2/sum w^2`, maximum weight, and cell counts. Do not self-normalize weights while calling the estimator unbiased.

The target population loss is `||Sigma_*-I||_F^2/16`. Squaring one empirical matrix adds a variance term. For the inferential arms, use two independent mini-batches `A,D` and

\[
\widehat L_{\rm pool}={1\over16}
\operatorname{tr}[(\widehat\Sigma_A-I)(\widehat\Sigma_D-I)].
\]

Each matrix uses the exact weights above. Independence gives the intended population loss in expectation; both branches receive gradients. This cross estimator may be negative for a finite batch. Log a separate, nonnegative large-panel defect for diagnosis. The global control uses the same estimator structure with `w=1`, so weighting is the only difference between those two arms.

This resolves Audit Issue #7's distribution-estimation defect. It does not alone resolve conditional rank collapse.

### 2.3 Add the missing conditional term

The recommended objective is

\[
L_{\rm orth}=L_{\rm pool}+\lambda_{\rm cfg}L_{\rm cfg},
\quad L_{\rm cfg}={1\over144}\sum_k{1\over16}\|\Sigma_k-I\|_F^2,
\quad\lambda_{\rm cfg}=1.
\]

For uniform configurations,

\[
{1\over144}\sum_k\|\Sigma_k-I\|_F^2
=\|\Sigma_*-I\|_F^2+
{1\over144}\sum_k\|\Sigma_k-\Sigma_*\|_F^2.
\]

The second term explicitly penalizes between-configuration second-moment imbalance. Neither pooled weighting nor a single global whitening matrix supplies it. The combined objective intentionally gives pooled fit additional weight.

Implement the conditional estimator by drawing one `k` uniformly per update and drawing two independent conditional micro-batches of 64 outcomes each, with scene probabilities `alpha` and partition probabilities `beta`. Their cross-moment loss is unbiased for that configuration's squared defect, and uniform selection is unbiased for the 144-group mean. Additional configurations can be processed sequentially without retaining 720 computation graphs. Every arm performs the same number of encoder evaluations; unused regularizer samples in controls contribute zero loss. Global halves contain 256 samples each.

Use the audited FB loss unchanged, normalized by the same frozen convention across arms, and add this dimensionless orthogonal loss with coefficient one. Ramp its coefficient linearly from zero to one over the first 1,000 updates; freeze it thereafter. No validation-based coefficient search is allowed within this spike. An unsuccessful coefficient is reported as an unsuccessful pre-registered intervention, not evidence that all conditional methods fail.

Do **not** force every scene-conditioned or static-hold-only matrix to equal `I`. A point-mass equilibrium necessarily has second-moment rank one; finite-support distributions have corresponding support-rank limits. Conditioning on an E3 hold can make isotropy impossible even when the encoder correctly preserves the outcome. Scene/capacity matrices and all 720 cell spectra are diagnostics, while the `>=12` hard gate is on each of the 144 broad, pre-specified configuration distributions. Insufficient broad support is recorded, not concealed by pooling configurations or adding jitter. Simultaneous conditional isotropy across different distributions is a hypothesis, not something a finite penalty guarantees.

### 2.4 (c) Nondimensionalized g13 plus a recoverable scale ratio

Dividing all tip coordinates by length removes useful scale information unless length is retained. Appending a fourteenth input is also disallowed. The canonical rotary geometry supplies an exactly redundant slot: in housing frame `M`, `v_tip,z = 0` because `v=omega u cross p` and `u=(0,0,1)`.

Keep raw physical `g13` as the external interface. Inside `B`, apply this fixed transform before the existing 13-input MLP:

\[
L(g)=\|p\|_2,\quad L_*=0.20\ {\rm m},\quad T_*=0.10\ {\rm s},\quad r_L=L(g)/L_*,
\]

\[
T(g)=\left[
\cos q,\sin q,T_*\omega,T_*^2a,
{p_x\over L},{p_y\over L},{p_z\over L},
{T_*v_x\over L},{T_*v_y\over L},r_L,
{T_*^2\hat a_x\over L},{T_*^2\hat a_y\over L},{T_*^2\hat a_z\over L}
\right].
\]

The zero-based slot formerly carrying `v_z` is slot 9. All 13 transformed values are dimensionless; `r_L` lies in `[0.4,2.25]` over the frozen length range. The scale comes only from the measured/computed single-step tip position already in `g13`; it is not fetched from `p5065`, a configuration lookup, or `x137`.

The inverse on the canonical kinematic manifold is explicit: recover `L=L_*r_L`, divide angular entries by `T_*` and `T_*^2`, multiply normalized position by `L`, normalized velocity by `L/T_*`, and normalized acceleration by `L/T_*^2`, and restore `v_z=0`. Thus physical lever arm, linear velocity, tangential acceleration, and centripetal acceleration are retained exactly in the input transformation. Actual momentum still requires mass, which `g13` did not contain originally; no momentum-observability claim is added.

For matched angular trajectories and normalized tip geometry, the normalized dynamic coordinates now coincide across lengths, while a separate scalar retains scale. This removes artificial multiplication of all nine tip channels by length. It does not force the nonlinear encoder to retain that scale, equalize different stroke distributions, or produce equal covariances. Physical-decoding and successor gates test whether it does useful work.

**Manifest and deployment guards.** Before using this arm, verify that the actual locked layout is the canonical kinematic 13-field layout, and that tip velocity is computed as the housing-relative rigid-joint velocity in the baseline. A variant that replaces a coordinate with effort/margin, or a future moving-parent absolute-velocity representation, needs a separately adjudicated transform; it must not silently discard its ninth slot. For this spike, fail the transformed arm with `SCHEMA_INCOMPATIBLE` if the contract is absent. Check `L>0` and the redundant-slot identity against FP64 recomputation, with relative/absolute tolerance `1e-6` in the stated SI units. Do not quietly divide by an epsilon and treat an invalid length as valid data.

The same transform is applied to reached outcomes, desired outcomes, replay outcomes, and target-network inputs. Raw outcomes remain stored so preprocessing can be reproduced. Fixed calibration constants and the existing normalizer's nontrainable statistics are serialized and byte-counted. The neural tensors, dimensions, and parameter counts remain unchanged. Fit and freeze normalizers from balanced rule/policy training data under B02's contract; no test-statistics refresh or per-configuration whitening is permitted.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| (a) Raw inputs, global loss | Only if sampling already matches target | No guarantee | Present in raw inputs | Can pass while every group has rank one |
| (b) Raw inputs, IPW pooled loss | Yes, under support and exact-probability premises | No guarantee | Present in raw inputs | Correct mixture is still only a mixture |

## 3. Serious Alternative Considered and Rejected

**Alternative: balance the replay, train one global encoder, and whiten its final output with a single shared matrix.** This is attractive because it is inexpensive and appears to make the representation isotropic without noisy per-configuration estimates. One could fit `A=Sigma_*^(-1/2)` on training data, transform `B` by `A`, and transform forward factors by the inverse transpose to preserve bilinear products before sphere normalization.

It is rejected as the solution to the stated problem for three reasons. First, any invertible common `A` preserves the algebraic rank of each `Sigma_k`; it cannot repair a truly rank-one conditional representation. In the explicit 144-group counterexample, `Sigma_*=I` already, so the whitening matrix is the identity. Second, pointwise sphere reprojection after whitening is nonlinear and generally destroys both exact whitening and the exact bilinear product-preservation argument. Third, per-configuration whitening would require a deployment-time selector and distinct coordinate systems for goals; it changes the shared interface and does not solve unobservable distinctions among outcomes that map to the same `g13`.

This rejection is narrow: global whitening can improve conditioning and is a reasonable diagnostic. It cannot be a certificate of per-configuration effective rank or semantic utility. The proposed conditional penalty may still fail; the spike is designed to reveal that failure.

A second tempting shortcut, `p/L, v/L, a/L` without a scale carrier, is rejected because two different lengths with otherwise matched normalized kinematics become exactly indistinguishable to `B`. Source-side geometry in `F` does not restore a missing target-outcome distinction. The explicit `r_L` construction avoids that loss without adding inputs or parameters.

## 4. Candidate Necessity Claims

These are **Candidate** claims, not accepted principles or empirical findings. Grade descriptions below are proposed mathematical scopes, subject to adjudication.

### C-B03-B-01 — Pooled isotropy cannot certify conditional resolution

**Proposed grade:** Grade 1, mathematical counterexample. **Premises:** 144 positive-mass configuration distributions; a deterministic shared measurable radius-four encoder on outcome supports that can distinguish the constructed groups; a guarantee based only on the pooled second moment being `I16`.

**Claim:** Those premises do not imply any per-configuration effective-rank lower bound above one. Therefore a certification claim of `erank(Sigma_k)>=12` for every configuration needs conditional evidence or an independently proved structural restriction that excludes conditional collapse. A particular conditional loss is not logically necessary; the conditional information is.

**Proof:** Assign nine configurations to each of 16 axes and symmetric outputs `+/-4e_j` within each assigned group. All 144 conditional second moments have rank one, while their uniform average is `I16`. Exact target-law IPW leaves this example unchanged. This proves the impossibility of the pooled-only inference.

**Boundary and falsification route:** Identical conditional outcome laws would force identical conditional moments and exclude this example. A proof of such an additional physical restriction could replace conditional measurement; none appears in the frozen context. A numerical implementation that does not reproduce pooled rank 16 and group rank one on the specified synthetic construction falsifies the implementation, not this algebraic result. Physics results with all groups healthy do not invalidate the claim; they show that the counterexample did not occur there.

### C-B03-B-02 — A shared 16-dimensional right span imposes a spectral tail floor

**Proposed grade:** Grade 1, conditional linear-algebra statement. **Premises:** a fixed common outcome basis/measure, common operator target, fixed row weights, and bilinear predictions with a right factor of width 16. **Claim:** relative projected successor error cannot be below `epsilon_16,*` from Section 1.3. If measured target spectra robustly place that floor above `0.20`, no change to weighting or normalization can achieve the charter's error threshold on that same panel while keeping this factorization.

**Proof:** The predicted stack has rank at most 16; orthogonal projection onto the leading singular directions minimizes squared Frobenius residual, leaving precisely the squared tail. The orthogonal-right-mode specialization gives `0.942809...`; mere Frobenius orthogonality of blocks does not.

**Boundary:** A finite projected spectrum yields only a lower bound for the tested target. Monte Carlo noise can inflate the estimated tail. No impossibility finding is issued without independent branch estimates and uncertainty bounds. Nonlinear input coordinates can help the network learn a better basis but do not change the fixed raw-outcome target's mathematical rank.

### C-B03-B-03 — Removing all length carriers makes physical tip scale unrecoverable

**Proposed grade:** Grade 1 on the matched-kinematics family. **Premises:** two rigid configurations with identical normalized tip geometry and `(q,omega,a)`, unequal lengths, and a backward input containing normalized kinematics but no recoverable length carrier. **Claim:** no deterministic decoder of that input alone can recover both physical tip positions correctly. The input collision is exact while the target positions differ by `(L1-L2)rhat`.

The proposed transform avoids this specific collision through an explicit inverse. This proves preservation by preprocessing, not preservation by the learned 16-dimensional embedding. The latter remains an empirical claim tested below.

Only the final checkpoint is confirmatory. Optimizer settings and FB scaling are taken from the audited common implementation and recorded before launching any arm. Missing configuration values block launch rather than inviting per-arm tuning. No test-driven early stopping, normalizer refresh, or checkpoint selection.

### 5.2 Data partitions and matrix diagnostics

Separate training, reference calibration, validation, and test by complete episode and disturbance-generator seed. For each `(k,e)` test cell, collect 160 independent valid physics episodes and preselect one control boundary per episode using a fixed seeded rule. Allocate rule/policy episodes in the frozen `1:2` reference mixture using weights when integer allocations do not divide exactly. Within each cell, cover both load signs and pre-specified onset/hold/release phases where applicable. A failed episode is logged and cannot be replaced silently. Cap attempted episodes at twice the requested count per cell; otherwise report `UNDERPOWERED` with reason codes.

Compute all 144 conditional spectra in FP64 using the exact target scene/partition weights, not their realized sample frequencies. Report uncentered and centered effective rank, all 16 eigenvalues, mean norm, centered trace, `||Sigma_k-I||_F^2/16`, and numerical rank with tolerance `1e-8 * lambda_max`. Do not add a ridge before the rank calculation. Roundoff-sized negative eigenvalues may be set to zero after logging their magnitude; substantive negative values are an estimator error.

Also report spectra for all 720 `(k,e)` cells, 15 scene-capacity groups, each stroke, and short/middle/long length bins defined by training-manifest tertiles. They diagnose where an intervention fails; they do not replace the 144-group gates. The static-hold subset is reported separately without the impossible demand that a single equilibrium have 12 independent directions.

Negative controls are a constant radius-four encoder and the 144-group axis construction. A deterministic high-frequency encoder independent of training outcomes serves as a semantic negative control: it may attain high rank, but must not be accepted without successor and decoding success. Repeated identical `g` must produce identical `B(g)`; dropout and test-time noise are disabled. Test resolution includes the calibrated sensor quantization, so rank supported only by sub-resolution numerical jitter is not accepted.

### 5.3 Independent successor reference and worst-group error

Build a common finite outcome dictionary on a **separate physical calibration split**, never from an arm's embedding. Scale raw SI outcomes by the fixed `L_*` and `T_*` units solely for dictionary construction; retain length in the target. Fit 128 deterministic Voronoi outcome cells using fixed seeded initialization. Require each calibration cell to have at least 64 outcomes; otherwise increase calibration collection within a pre-registered doubling cap of four times the initial budget, then report `UNDERPOWERED`. Do not reduce the dictionary to 16 or merge bins based on test error.

Let `nu_j>0` be the frozen calibration probability of cell `j` and set `phi_j(g)=1[g in cell j]/sqrt(nu_j)`. These functions are orthonormal under that reference. The backward moment matrix is computed on the same frozen calibration measure, `A_B(:,j)=E[B(g)phi_j(g)]`. The partition and its probabilities are identical for all arms. This tests coarse physical outcome occupancy, rather than using a learned embedding as its own target.

For each configuration and scene, select two independent test source states, three initial normalized actions `{-0.8,0,0.8}`, and two fixed radius-four latent vectors with opposite first components. This yields 60 rows per configuration and 8,640 rows in the stack. Source rows come from genuine physics states with correctly initialized causal histories. Give scenes total weights `alpha_e`, uniform weights to rows within scene, and equal weight to each configuration.

For each row, estimate discounted occupancy with 32 independent simulator continuations under the common reference policy, split into two independent sets of 16. All branches use B01-compliant causal context at the source. Future realized contacts can enter the measured target, but never the source inputs. The truncation mass is bounded by `gamma^H`; additionally bound the feature-vector tail by `gamma^H/sqrt(min_j nu_j)` and extend `H` before inference if this exceeds `0.01` of the calibration reference norm.

Compare the measured target `M_k` with predictions `F_k A_B`, including the exact discount-normalization conversion. Define

\[
E_k={\|W_k^{1/2}(M_k-\widehat M_k)\|_F\over
\|W_k^{1/2}M_k\|_F},\qquad E_{\rm worst}=\max_k E_k.
\]

Use independent branch estimates to report target disagreement, Monte Carlo uncertainty, and cross-estimated squared residuals. A nonpositive noise-corrected denominator or unresolved error floor yields `UNDERPOWERED`, not zero error. If needed, the sole permitted expansion is from 32 to 128 continuations per row, using new seeds for every arm equally. No further adaptive collection is allowed in the confirmatory spike.

Report the stack's complete singular spectrum, cumulative energy through rank 16, and per-configuration spectra. Calculate both the oracle rank-16 stack residual and each arm's actual residual. Report independent-half spectral disagreement and bootstrap perturbation bounds on singular values; a noisy positive tail alone cannot certify a physical rank floor. Require the lower uncertainty bound on the tail ratio to exceed `0.20` before issuing `RANK_LIMIT_FAIL`; otherwise unresolved spectral evidence is `UNDERPOWERED`. For a fixed learned `A_B`, also compute the minimum-residual left factor separately for each evaluation row: this is an explicitly optimistic projection diagnostic of the learned right span. Evaluate its stability using the independent branch halves so reference noise cannot masquerade as basis failure. Never call this oracle the deployed forward model or a generalizing predictor.

**Successor gate:** the simultaneous 95% upper confidence bound on `E_worst` must be strictly below `0.20`, in each of the three seeds. The projected oracle tail must be compatible with that threshold. A clearly larger oracle tail is `RANK_LIMIT_FAIL`; a small oracle tail with large learned error is `FIT_FAIL` or `BASIS_FAIL`, depending on the fixed-basis oracle. A small finite-panel tail does not prove the full infinite-dimensional H3 claim.

### 5.4 Zero-shot physical outcome decoding and scale preservation

Freeze each `B` before fitting a training-only affine ridge probe from `b16` to the original physical 13 fields. This is a diagnostic of linearly available physical semantics, not an added deployed component. Standardize output units with the same fixed constants, use ridge penalty `1e-3` on weights in the mean-squared training objective, and do not penalize the intercept. Fit probes on training outcomes only. Also fit constant-output and untrained-encoder probes as controls.

The main test decodes new episodes and new continuous target outcomes in all 144 configurations. In addition, pre-register a morphology holdout: within each capacity tier, sort geometry seeds by length and deterministic seed tie-break; withhold the shortest, lower-median, and longest seed, with all four strokes. Provided the manifest contains at least three distinct seeds per tier and leaves training geometries in every tier, this holds out nine geometries and 36 configurations. If it does not, register a corrected stratified split before any run. Retrain W, WS, and NWS from scratch on the remaining 108 configurations, using the same three seeds and 12,000 updates. All their normalizers, dictionary calibration, probe fitting, and FB training exclude the held-out geometries. For this experiment only, replace training-law factors `1/144` by `1/108`, recompute `N_c` on the training subset, and sample the conditional loss solely over training configurations; withheld cells are not missing training support. Evaluation weights are separately normalized over the 36 held-out configurations. Held-out physical outcomes appear only at evaluation. This is the explicit zero-shot morphology test; the full-144 experiment alone is not one.

Gate on **each held-out configuration**, pooling scenes with `alpha`: circular angle RMSE at most 5 degrees; angular velocity RMSE at most `0.10/T_* = 1 rad/s`; angular acceleration RMSE at most `0.10/T_*^2 = 10 rad/s^2`; vector RMSE at most `0.10 L_* = 0.02 m` for tip position, `0.10 L_*/T_* = 0.2 m/s` for tip velocity, and `0.10 L_*/T_*^2 = 2 m/s^2` for tip acceleration. Vector RMSE uses Euclidean error per example, not averaging over three coordinates. Require decoded length `||p_decoded||` to have relative RMS error at most 5%. Report each field separately; an aggregate score cannot hide failure of the scale carrier. Invalid decoded angle pairs count as failures rather than being dropped.

Apply the same per-group rank gates below to the 36 held-out configurations. Success establishes transfer on this registered split, not arbitrary unseen robot designs. The affine-probe gate is deliberately restrictive: a failure means linear semantic accessibility has not been demonstrated, not that no nonlinear decoder could exist.

### 5.5 Hard gates, uncertainty, and disposition

Use 2,000 paired episode-bootstrap replicates, preserving cells and resampling independent branch units for successor uncertainty. Compute simultaneous bounds by bootstrapping the minimum rank or maximum error across groups directly. Record the bootstrap seed. All three training seeds must pass individually; averaged seeds cannot hide a failing configuration. Thresholds are design choices fixed here, not claimed measured values.

| Gate | Pre-registered requirement |
|---|---|
| Integrity | Actual physics provenance, all 720 cells present, B01 causality audit, B02 event/witness prerequisites, fixed test hashes, no test leakage |
| Architecture | Exactly `d_z=16`, `B=74,000`, actor `207,617`, total `281,617`; exported dependency audit shows only raw g13 for B and x137/z16 for actor |
| Conditional rank | **Every one of 144 groups has `erank(Sigma_k) >= 12`, equivalently `erank(Sigma_k)/16 >= 12/16`**, and the simultaneous 95% lower bound on the minimum is at least 12 |
| Centered check | Every broad configuration distribution has centered effective rank at least 12, centered trace at least 8, with simultaneous lower bounds; no mean-only or near-constant code acceptance |
| Successor | Simultaneous upper bound on worst-group relative error `<0.20` |
| Zero-shot semantics | All registered physical-field and length thresholds pass on each of the 36 morphology-holdout configurations, with simultaneous upper bounds |
| Zero-shot rank | All 36 morphology-holdout groups meet the same uncentered and centered rank/trace gates |
| Stability | No invalid transform, nonfinite weight, hidden missing cell, encoder jitter injection, or unsupported normalizer change |

The centered rank/trace gate is an additional conservative requirement; the theorem in Section 1 concerns the uncentered second moment and does not imply this gate. A regularizer can therefore pass its uncentered target and still fail the practical spike.

For intervention attribution, report paired changes in worst-group error and minimum conditional rank for `G/W`, `W/WS`, and `WS/NWS`. Claim improvement only when the paired 95% interval excludes zero in the claimed direction; report absolute gate success separately. If W already passes all gates, conditional loss is not empirically necessary on this panel; retain W unless a more complex passing arm has a supported benefit. Otherwise, if WS passes and NWS provides no supported benefit, retain WS to avoid unnecessary preprocessing changes. If NWS alone passes, it is the selected candidate, conditional on the stated scope and schema guard. These rules never waive a failed gate in exchange for a relative improvement.

Archive the proposal hash, manifests, calibration transforms, episode provenance, sampling probabilities, physical dictionary, per-group spectra, probe coefficients, bootstrap results, parameter audit, and a machine-readable verdict. Allowed verdicts are `PASS_REPRESENTATION_SPIKE`, `FAIL`, `UNDERPOWERED`, and prerequisite/schema failure codes. Do not mark a surrogate or an analytic test as `coverage_ready` for physics. Static-hold metrics remain genuine rollout metrics with fixed equilibrium targets and B02 guards; this fixed-policy representation spike cannot establish learned-actor zero-velocity convergence.

The decisive result is the conjunction of conditional rank, independent successor accuracy, and retained physical semantics under a fixed deployment budget. No single covariance statistic substitutes for that conjunction.
