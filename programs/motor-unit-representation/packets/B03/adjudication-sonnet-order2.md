## 4. Disposition

**Recommend: Tie.** Q leads by 4.0 points (76 vs 72), inside the 5.0 margin.

**Alternate**: **Q** is the stronger base, because it leads on the heaviest rubric dimension. P should be treated as co-primary. P's estimator specification and statistical protocol are better. If one candidate must be the carrier of the merged proposal, carry **Q** and apply the merge items below.

**Why it is a tie and not a win for Q**: Q's advantage is almost entirely in the 30% dimension (the disjoint-shell analysis), where P is weaker. Q's own spike has two critical flaws (S1, S2), and its feature-model table has D1. P has a stronger Issue #7 estimator and a better statistical design. The two candidates share one important flaw: both recommend a conditional `Σ_k → I` target that conflicts with scale-preserving decoding.

### Mandatory Merge Items

1. **Conditional reference-measure lemma (from Q, with P's caveats).** Adopt the Ky Fan floor and the disjoint-support corollary as a *conditional* lemma with explicit regularity assumptions. Do not lock the 0.745 figure or "H3 impossible". Require a premise audit (are the 36 lever arms distinct, and is the V3 reference pooled?).
2. **Target-space repair in the spike (both candidates, critical).** Define the successor target on a *common measurable partition of physical outcome space*. Do not use per-configuration cells joined as a disjoint union (Q), and do not use a single coarse pooled dictionary that is either blind to length or floor-bound (P). Report the pooled-reference and configuration-restricted errors separately. Add a small known-spectrum shell-separable toy problem as an implementation check.
3. **Reference-measure arms.** Add at least a configuration-matched reference arm and a support-class or partially local reference arm (`ν_{k,ε}`) as pre-registered comparators. This makes pooled-vs-matched a tested contrast and not an assumption.
4. **Replace `‖Σ_k − I‖² → 0` with a rank-floor conditional penalty.** Use an eigenvalue hinge, or a Rényi-2 floor `tr(Σ_k²) ≤ 64/3` (which implies erank ≥ 12). Re-derive the compatibility of the length and tip decoding gates with the new optimum. Drop or quantify the λ-anchor tradeoff (Q) and the affine-probe conflict (P).
5. **Estimator specification (from P into Q's design).** Use exact hierarchical sampler-probability weights, no clipping beyond the registered target-law mixture, no silent stratum dropping (use `INSUFFICIENT_SUPPORT` or an explicit bias log), and the two-batch cross estimator. Report PR from the same plug-in matrix on both sides of the identity, with the U-based estimate reported separately. Register many configurations per update, not one.
6. **Gate corrections.**
   - Remove G3's "bound ≥ 0.70" and the A4-shuf matched-error ≥ 0.70 requirement, and the decision rule that treats A1/A2 success as a falsifier of C-B03-Q-01. Keep only an algebraic consistency test on the same weighted finite operator.
   - Remove G6's "W must fail". Let the whitening control pass or fail on its merits. Correct the §3 claim that whitening trivially yields PR = 16 after sphere reprojection.
   - Make decoding thresholds length-relative.
   - Keep PARTIAL (≤ 0.30) labelled as exploratory, not as satisfying the charter's < 0.20.
7. **Statistics.** At least 10–20 source states per (k, e) cell (P's 2 is degenerate). Simultaneous bounds by bootstrapping the min or max across groups. Vary z along several directions in the reference policy. Treat truncation error explicitly (Q: about 4.8% at H = 150, γ = 0.98). Use independent calibration and evaluation splits.
8. **Scene-cell gating.** Add a pass-critical criterion for E1/E3/E4 boundary and load cells (decoding or prediction error). Do not require rank ≥ 12 on genuinely degenerate hold distributions.
9. **Ablation hygiene.** Isolate `T` from the normalizer (add a "WS + balanced normalization" arm, P) and `T` from the anchor (A3 vs A4−λ, Q). Rename Q's A4−λ, which is not a ρ-blind arm. Include a matched-reference/global-orthogonality comparison.
10. **Budget audit.** Resolve Q's 70,800 vs 74,000 discrepancy with an instantiated module inventory, and state the convention for non-trainable buffers (P's byte-counting of normalizer state).
11. **Held-out contracts.** Q: training-only schedule for 112 configurations (no held-out data in FB, orthogonality, anchor, κ selection or normalization). P: report interpolation and extrapolation holdouts separately.
12. **Claim statuses** as in §3.

### Confounds

- **Presentation order.** I scored the files in the order given (P then Q). I did not run a swapped-order pass, so an order effect cannot be excluded. The 4.0-point gap is small enough that it could be an artifact.
- **Length, density and style.** Q is longer, more theorem-dense and has more tables. Candidates that look more rigorous can attract higher scores, and I tried to correct for this by checking claims directly (below). I found real defects in Q (D1, S1, S2), which I scored down.
- **Asymmetric critique severity.** Review A (on P) found two critical and six major issues. Review B (on Q) found four critical-level items (M1, T1, S1, S2) and several majors. The two reviewers may differ in strictness. I weighed each objection on its mathematical merit and not by count. Two objections cut both ways. P's critique shows that P has the pooled-reference floor problem, and Q's critique shows that Q's spike manufactures a floor.
- **Unverified premises.** I did not read the baseline documents or decision records. The pooled FB reference in V3, the distinctness of the 36 lever arms, the exact `g13` layout (a dead `v_z` slot) and the 74,000 count are taken from the candidates. The truth of the "0.745 under V3" claim hinges on the first two.
- **What I checked myself.**
  - Q's block-diagonal floor √(1 − 64/144) ≈ 0.745, and the matching P-critique figure √(1 − 16/36) ≈ 0.745.
  - P's erank bound at δ² = 16/3 (erank ≥ 12).
  - The B parameter sum of 70,800.
  - The logic of the `Σ_k = I` vs affine-decodability conflict.
  - The logic of the sphere-omission defect in Q §1.4.
  - I did not rerun any numerical experiment, and I did not recompute Q's synthetic tables.
- **Surrogate vs physics.** P is blocked on physics integration, and Q is runnable on a reduced surrogate. I did not score on executability, because the rubric does not ask for it. If the program weights executability, that favors Q.
- **Rubric ambiguity.** The brief's Dim 4 asks only for pre-registered per-group effective-rank thresholds, and both candidates meet it literally. Scores in that row reflect joint satisfiability and inferential validity, which is my reading of "falsifiability". A stricter literal reading would raise both scores by a similar amount and would not change the ranking.
- **Tie margin edge.** The gap (4.0) is inside the margin by 1.0 point. A one-point re-weighting of a single row (for example, scoring Q's D1 defect as more severe) would remove Q's lead entirely, and a different weighting of the 30% row could move it past 5. I recommend the merge in §4 and not a winner-take-all pick.
